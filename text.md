# Film Making Algorithm

**Audience:** First-year film students who are new to the filmmaking process.
**Purpose:** To introduce the five basic stages every film goes through, from idea to release, so you can see how the pieces fit together before studying each stage in depth.

## Main Steps in the Filmmaking Process
1. Development
2. Pre-Production
3. Production (Principal Photography)
4. Post-Production
5. Distribution

---

## 1. Development

In this stage, a film starts as an idea and ends as a script that someone has agreed to fund.

- A writer turns an idea into a script: an original screenplay, an adaptation of a book, or a short pitch document.
- The writer revises the script through several drafts, often based on notes from a producer or studio.
- A producer or studio buys the rights to the script and starts approaching investors, studios, or financiers to fund production.

**If financing falls through:** the project can stall for months or years, or move to a different producer. This is normal and does not mean the project has failed.

---

## 2. Pre-Production

*This is the planning stage. It is usually the longest part of the process, because every decision made here saves time and money once filming starts.*

- **Budgeting** — The producer calculates how much the film will cost and secures the funding to cover it.
- **Casting** — The director and casting director select actors for lead and supporting roles, usually through auditions.
- **Hiring the crew** — The producer hires key department heads: the director of photography (DP), production designer, costume designer, editor, and composer.
- **Location scouting** — The location manager finds and secures real filming locations, or the production team builds sets.
- **Storyboarding and shot lists** — The director and DP plan each scene visually, sometimes using simple sketches or animated previews (previz).
- **Scheduling** — The assistant director creates a shooting schedule. Scenes are grouped by location or by which actors are available — they are almost never shot in the order they appear in the story.
- **Permits and insurance** — The production team files for shooting permits with local authorities, buys production insurance, and rents equipment such as cameras, lighting, and grip gear.

**If a location becomes unavailable:** the production team scouts a replacement and adjusts the schedule. Losing a location late in pre-production is common and rarely stops a film.

---

## 3. Production (Principal Photography)

This is the stage where the crew actually films the movie.

A typical shooting day includes:
1. Setting up lights and camera for the scene.
2. Rehearsing blocking (where actors move and stand).
3. Shooting multiple takes of each shot.
4. Capturing coverage — wide shots, close-ups, and reaction shots — so editors have options later.

While this happens:
- The sound recordist records dialogue separately on set (production sound), to be synced with picture later.
- The script supervisor tracks continuity, so details like clothing or prop position stay consistent between takes.
- The director works with the DP to achieve the visual style planned in pre-production.

Most productions review the **dailies** — the raw, unedited footage from that day, every evening to catch problems early.

**If a shot doesn't work:** the crew reshoots it on the spot, or the director adds it to a list of scenes to re-shoot later (a pickup day).

---

## 4. Post-Production

This is where all the raw material becomes a finished film.

- **Editing** — The editor assembles a rough cut from all the footage, then works with the director to refine it into a final cut.
- **Sound design** — The sound team adds sound effects, ADR (dialogue re-recorded in a studio), Foley (custom-recorded sound effects like footsteps), and ambient background sound.
- **Music** — A composer writes an original score, or the production licenses existing music for the soundtrack.
- **Visual effects (VFX)** — VFX artists add CGI, composite shots together, and apply color correction and color grading.
- **Mixing** — The sound mixer balances dialogue, music, and effects into the final audio track.

**If the edit isn't working:** the director and editor can restructure scenes, cut material, or in rare cases reshoot — this is a normal part of shaping the story.

---

## 5. Distribution

This is where the finished film reaches an audience.

- **Marketing** — The distributor creates trailers and posters, organizes press tours, and submits the film to festivals.
- **Delivery** — The post team exports the finished film into the technical formats each platform requires (for example, a DCP file for cinemas, or specific codecs for streaming platforms.)
- **Release** — The film opens in theaters, on a streaming platform, or both, followed by later release in secondary markets such as digital rental or home video.

---

## Quick Reference

| Stage | Main Question It Answers |
|---|---|
| Development | Is this story worth funding? |
| Pre-Production | How will we make this, and with what? |
| Production | How do we capture the footage? |
| Post-Production | How do we shape the footage into a film? |
| Distribution | How does the film reach an audience? |
